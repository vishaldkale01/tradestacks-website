04 / POSITION PROTECTION

Built to define risk.
Not eliminate it.

The code includes controls for capital exposure and active trade management. These are software mechanisms, not guarantees against loss or slippage.

01 / SESSION

Daily drawdown lock

Redis-backed risk state can block new entries after a configured daily-loss boundary. The gateway default specifies 5% of configured capital.

02 / ENTRY

Capital & liquidity checks

Position-sizing calculations and option-spread/depth conditions are considered before simulated execution.

03 / POSITION

Stop and profit management

The lifecycle component contains initial stop, break-even, partial milestone, trailing and target-state logic.

04 / RESILIENCE

Missing price protection

The risk engine includes a block for unresolved exit pricing, keeping new entries restricted until reconciliation.

Configuration values and enforcement differ by component and strategy version. The website does not claim the previously discussed “two-loss guard” is verified across all paths; that requires a separate runtime audit.
05 / TRADE LIFECYCLE

What happens
after a paper entry?

The trade context tracks index-based stop/target levels while preserving option fill and P&L details. A trade is monitored until an exit reason is recorded.

ENTRY

Start position

Simulated order context and entry conditions are recorded.

PROTECT

Move stop

Configured break-even and profit-lock milestones may adjust index-based protection.

FOLLOW

Trail or exit

Trailing, stop, target and adverse momentum behavior are evaluated by the position logic.

REVIEW

Close and audit

Exit reason, fill data and estimated fees become part of the recorded trade history.

Exit reasons and P&L attribution must be reconciled against the actual data. Labels such as PROFIT_LOCK and BREAK_EVEN should not be taken as proof of a profitable close without the recorded prices and fees.
06 / REPLAY ENGINE

Replay the inputs.
Question the outcome.

The engine records tick and depth streams into date-partitioned Parquet data. Replay uses a simulation clock and a depth-aware fill model to inspect historical behavior.

01

Capture

Asynchronous market-data recording and session manifests.

02

Synchronize

Use a simulated clock instead of mixing live wall time into historical events.

03

Model fills

Evaluate order-book depth, timing and estimated transaction charges.

04

Validate

Check gaps, completeness and differences between replay and paper behavior.
